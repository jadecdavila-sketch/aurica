## 01 · The Thesis

> ***Sketch + Hammer builds AI for humans.***
>
> *We take products from good to great through a methodology that is rigorous, human-led, and structured to prevent the slow drift toward generic that kills most products before they know they are dying.*

Most products drift toward generic - not through one catastrophic decision but through hundreds of small ones, each defensible, each reaching for the safe default because the default is faster than the specific choice. Generic UX heuristics override product-specific principles. Features ship that look right and fail their actual user.

The North Star is what prevents that drift. It names the product's thesis precisely enough that every decision - design, engineering, content, AI recommendation - can be tested against it. When technical correctness and the North Star diverge, the North Star wins.

## 03 · Who We Build For

We build for founders and organizations who understand that the difference between a product that works and a product that is trusted is not a feature set - it is a coherent point of view, held precisely, built into the structure of the work from day one.

### The client we are built for

- Knows what they are building and why it matters to a specific person
- Understands that AI capability without human judgment produces confident, generic output
- Wants the methodology, not just the output - willing to learn alongside us
- Values process rigour as a competitive advantage, not overhead
- Is building something that needs to be trusted, not just used

### The client we are not built for

- Wants AI to replace judgment rather than extend it
- Measures success by speed of feature delivery alone
- Treats the North Star as optional documentation rather than the binding constraint
- Is unwilling to be in the loop for critical decisions

## 04 · The Methodology

### The durodango principle

Every engagement begins with raw material. Not a finished brief - what the client knows, what the user needs, what the product must do. Through patient, iterative work - discovery, validation, design, architecture, build, hardening, launch, learning, iteration, scale - that raw material is worked until it achieves its finish. The effort is not separate from the result. It is what creates it.

### The North Star - the binding constraint

Created at first engagement. Starts thin and true - the client's product thesis, their users, their brand, what the product must do and what it must refuse to be. Sharpened through every iteration. By the end of a serious engagement, it names something specific that did not exist when we began.

The thesis stays stable. The principles refine over time. The anti-patterns multiply as failure modes are observed and named. When technical correctness diverges from the North Star, the North Star wins.

### The Council - seven lenses, no votes

Seven specialized AI voices, each reading the product through its own lens, all held to the North Star. The Council does not vote. It surfaces tensions. Where voices disagree, the disagreement is the artifact - a decision the practitioner faces, named clearly, not flattened into a single compromised answer.

The practitioner decides which subset of the Council to invoke at each stage. Not all voices are needed at every moment. The human in the loop reads the North Star, decides which lenses to bring, and rules on what moves forward.

### The Foundation - living documentation

The architecture roadmap every AI agent reads before it touches a file. A short index routes to system-specific sub-docs. Each names its public interface, internal dependencies, gotchas, and known issues. Cross-system relationships are mapped explicitly. A pre-push workflow blocks code changes that do not touch the affected docs. The workflow is binding, not advisory.

For AI agents: the difference between confident, specific output and confidently wrong output. For human teams: the closest thing a small or distributed practice has to a code review for design work. For both: the discipline that lets the practice scale without losing awareness of what the codebase actually is.

## 05 · Design Principles

### What we insist on

- The product must be recognizably itself - not recognizably its category
- The human in the loop holds the final lens. The council surfaces; the practitioner decides
- Every AI recommendation is tested against the North Star before it is made
- Living documentation is not a best practice - it is the condition of quality at scale
- The system gets smarter after every session. Nothing is lost
- Auditable means the client can check our work. Trustworthy means they do not have to

### What we refuse

- Generic. At any stage, at any layer, for any reason
- AI output that has not been tested against the product's North Star
- Documentation that is advisory. The hook is binding or it does not exist
- Shortcuts that become walls. The Long Game asks: which shortcuts will we regret?
- Features that look right and fail their actual user

## 06 · Known Failure Modes - Anti-Patterns

These are the failure modes this North Star exists to catch. When a recommendation, a feature, or a decision triggers any of these, the North Star wins.

### In practice

- A feature is built that is technically correct but inconsistent with who the product is for
- Generic UX heuristics are applied without testing against the product's specific user
- An AI recommendation is accepted without testing it against the North Star
- Documentation is not updated when code changes - the living map becomes a lie
- The council is convened but its tensions are flattened into a single answer rather than named as a decision
- The North Star is treated as finished rather than as a thesis that sharpens over time

### In client relationships

- The client is not in the loop for critical decisions - they receive output without holding the lens
- The methodology is sold as a black box rather than something the client learns to use
- The engagement ends without the client owning the North Star and the Foundation

## 07 · The Practitioner's Statement

> *"Auditable means the client can check our work. Trustworthy means they don't have to."*
>
> This is what the practice is for. Not to impress with AI capability. To build products that are recognizably themselves, held to their thesis, trusted by the people they were built for - and to leave every client with the map, the methodology, and the knowledge of how to keep the work current long after the engagement ends.
